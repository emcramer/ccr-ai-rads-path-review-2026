DEVICE-LEVEL ADJUDICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A product code's regulation does not always describe the devices authorized
under it, and the mismatch is not random. FDA created the cancer-specific codes
only around 2018-2020, so before then mammography and lung CAD were authorized
under generic codes -- MYN holds the 1998 M1000 ImageChecker. A code-only rule
therefore undercounts as a FUNCTION OF TIME, which bends a trend line rather
than merely shortening it. Devices under generic codes are adjudicated one at a
time; see clinops.adjudication and config/adjudication/.

WHAT THIS COUNT IS NOT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An authorization is permission to market. It is not evidence of deployment,
of installed base, or of benefit, and nothing here speaks to how any of these
devices performs or whether it is used.

The count is also not a census of cancer-directed AI. Where a device could not
be resolved it was left out, and a clearance covering a whole imaging platform
is counted as one authorization even when the platform bundles a cancer CAD
feature among others. The figure counts AUTHORIZATIONS, not capabilities.
